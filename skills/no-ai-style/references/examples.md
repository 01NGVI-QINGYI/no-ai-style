# Worked rewrite pairs — illustrative, not new findings

Use these instructional examples to apply the gates. The "after" text
must not imply any fact missing from the "before" or other supplied
materials. No example is a substitute for checking the author's evidence.

## E1 · Gate 3 + Gate 4 · Remove empty lead-in (English)

**Before:** "It is important to note that the survey included 84 students."

**After:** "The survey included 84 students."

**Why:** The opening does not change evidence or meaning. Confirm the
number against the underlying survey before release (Gate 5.1).

## E2 · Gate 2 + Gate 4 · Distinguish precision from certainty (English)

**Before:** "Our results might possibly indicate an association between
documentation use and satisfaction scores."

**After:** "The results suggest an association between documentation use
and satisfaction scores."

**Why:** Redundant modals can be simplified without replacing association
with causation. If the author cannot support even "suggest," use `VERIFY`.

## E3 · Gate 2 · Do not upgrade exploratory findings (English)

**Before:** "In this exploratory sample, exposure was associated with
higher satisfaction scores."

**Unsafe after:** "Exposure improved satisfaction scores."

**Acceptable after:** "Exposure was associated with higher preparedness
scores in the exploratory sample."

**Why:** The unsafe version adds causality and drops scope.

## E4 · Gate 3 + Gate 4 · Direct statement (Chinese)

**Before:** "值得一提的是，本次调查共收集了84份有效问卷。"

**After:** "本次调查共收集84份有效问卷。"

**Why:** Removes a formulaic preface; keeps the count and respondent status.

## E5 · Gate 2 · Preserve limited inference (Chinese)

**Before:** "调查发现，使用说明文档与满意度得分呈正相关，但尚不能判断因果关系。"

**After:** "调查中，使用说明文档与满意度得分呈正相关；这一结果不构成因果证据。"

**Why:** Less defensive, but the causal caveat remains explicit.

## E6 · Gate 3 + Gate 4 · Japanese scholarly register

**Before:** "本研究においては、質問紙（しつもんし）調査を実施したということができる。"

**After:** "本研究では質問紙調査を実施した。"

**Why:** Deletes circumlocution; preserves completed-work tense. In a
published Japanese paper, furigana are normally omitted unless required.

## E7 · Gate 2 + Gate 4 · Japanese evidence restraint

**Before:** "結果から、両変数の関連が示唆される可能性があるかもしれない。"

**After:** "結果は、両変数の関連を示唆している。"

**Why:** Redundant uncertainty removed, but the result is still presented
as suggestive rather than causal. Check that the evidence supports it.

## E8 · Gate 3 + Gate 5.5 · Conversation residue versus history

**Conversation-only candidate:** The team discussed an unimplemented
classification algorithm, then decided on an SVM system.

**Bad deliverable title:** "The SVM System Without the Rejected Algorithm"

**Better title:** "SVM Classification System"

**Exception:** If an algorithm was previously shipped or formally used as
an experimental comparator, document that history where appropriate.

## E9 · Gate 2 + Gate 5.1 · Numeric protection

**Before:** "The improvement was 3.2 percentage points."

**Unsafe after:** "The improvement was 3.2%."

**Action:** Do not edit; percentage points and relative percentage changes
are not equivalent. Anchor checks may miss this semantic error.

## E10 · Gate 2 · Proposed versus completed work

**Before:** "We plan to recruit 50 participants in the next study."

**Unsafe after:** "We recruited 50 participants."

**After:** "For the next study, we plan to recruit 50 participants."

**Why:** Preserve the planned state, not merely the number.

## E11 · Gate 4 + Gate 5.4 · Keep useful repetition

**Input:** "The instrument records pressure. Pressure is reported in kPa."

**Decision:** Keep the repeated technical label. Replacing the second
"pressure" with "this phenomenon" would make the measurement less precise.
Repetition alone does not establish a reading problem.

## E12 · Gate 4 + Gate 5.4 · Check a phrase in context

**Input:** "For clarity, the archive covers April only."

**Revision:** "The archive covers April only."

**Similar passage to retain:** "The participants rated the instructions
for clarity."

The first passage has a removable lead-in. In the second, clarity names
what participants rated; the same words serve a different function.
