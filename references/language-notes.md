# Multilingual prose guidance

## English
Prefer an explicit subject and an action verb, but accept passive voice when the actor is unknown or the procedure matters most. Academic precision outweighs artificial informality. Repetition of a technical label may be clearer than endless synonyms. Keep `may`, `suggest`, `associated with`, and other uncertainty terms when evidence needs them.

## Chinese
Prefer a clear proposition before its supporting discussion. Avoid formulaic openings that merely proclaim a field's importance. Do not force every paragraph into a symmetrical three-part construction. Preserve disciplinary technical terms; do not replace precise concepts with elegant but ambiguous idioms. Distinguish writer stance (`笔者认为`) from evidence-backed findings (`研究表明`):
do not treat them as interchangeable or automatically delete either.
Differentiate association (`相关`, `有关`) from causation (`导致`, `引起`),
and retain warranted uncertainty (`可能`, `或许`, `似乎`, `倾向于`) rather
than deleting all hedges. A phrase such as `研究表明` requires actual
research support; it is not a stylistic substitute for `笔者认为`.

## Japanese
Choose a register for the whole document: scholarly `である` style is common for research plans and articles, while `です・ます` is suitable for many communications; follow local program and journal requirements. `示唆（しさ）する`, `考えられる`, and `可能性がある` can express necessary evidential restraint. Remove redundant clusters, not the evidential distinction. Preserve distinctions such as `検討（けんとう）した` (examined) vs `検証（けんしょう）した` (tested), `関連（かんれん）` (association) vs `因果（いんが）` (causation). Academic text need not insert furigana unless the user requests it.

## Translation across these languages
1. Extract proposition, evidence strength, modality, actors, time, and scope before translating.
2. Translate scientific meaning before adjusting register. Maintain fixed names, citations, numbers, and definitions.
3. Use target-language conventions to express the same degree of certainty, not mechanically identical modal words.
4. In 'literal translation' mode, do not add elegance, context, persuasive force, or connective claims absent from the source.
5. Back-compare the translation to the source for added or missing assertions.
